What I believe.

Great technology fails in the market every day. Not because it doesn't work, but because nobody made it legible to the person writing the check.

Most AI product failures are GTM failures dressed as technical failures. The build is usually fine. What's broken is who it's for, why to trust the output, and how the buyer is supposed to find it.

I'm opinion-forward. You'll hear what I think before I walk you through the trade-offs, and you'll hear what I won't do, and why, when something doesn't fit. No account managers. No layered teams. I'm the person in the room.

Selected work.

Figures are framed at order of magnitude and shown without employer attribution. Detail available under conversation.

Problem

Two buyer worlds were converging in real implementations but not in the product story: the legacy martech buyer and the warehouse-first data buyer working through activation partners. Selling without a joint story left the strongest use cases unclaimed.

Action

Led the repositioning in direct co-development with a warehouse-first activation partner, anchored on the operational outcomes customers were actually building. Designed and ran both halves of the partner motion, co-marketing and co-sell, not just the announcement most partnerships stop at, and built customer stories into the launch package as proof.

Outcome

A customer-validated repositioning shipped with customer stories in the launch package, and a co-sell motion that ran instead of sitting on a partnership page.

Problem

An AWS partnership existed on paper but no operating co-sell motion. The relationship was real; the pipeline wasn't. The opportunity was there, the operational design to capture it was not.

Action

Built the AWS co-sell program from the ground up. Structured the partner narrative so the product showed up inside the buyer conversations AWS account teams were already having. Aligned ISV partner managers and account executives on deal-stage handoffs and joint account planning, and ran the co-marketing that kept the motion compounding instead of spiking once and dying.

Outcome

A multi-million-dollar AWS revenue channel built from cold, with triple-digit year-over-year growth. A measurable channel, not a partnership announcement.

Problem

The platform sold to firms that had already standardized on Salesforce, but its narrative wasn't built to ride on top of Salesforce. The product sat next to a CRM the buyer had already chosen instead of making it more valuable.

Action

Built the positioning, product narrative, and sales motion inside the Salesforce ecosystem. Named Salesforce as the foundation rather than the competitor, and structured the joint value story around the relationship data Salesforce alone couldn't capture, so the conversation opened with extend what you have instead of replace what you have.

Outcome

Multi-million-dollar pipeline within 90 days of launch, on the company's first enterprise product.

Problem

A technically complex SDK product across web and mobile had churned through multiple PMs with no stable owner, and was pitched as a feature, which buried the real value: converting anonymous traffic the customer already paid to acquire into identifiable, activatable users.

Action

Covered functional PM scope for nine months. Ran MVP QA directly with engineering through beta, rewrote the technical docs, and repositioned the product from feature to revenue unlock with dual positioning: net-new acquisition for Sales, expansion for CS. Ran enablement for thirty reps that held up in technical conversations with enterprise architects, not just AE pitches, and managed the beta from four alpha to thirty beta customers.

Outcome

Roughly 20% expansion revenue tied to the motion, and it helped close the largest enterprise account in the company's history. The product converts up to 10% of a customer's anonymous traffic into activatable users.

Problem

A real-time data-sync product was heading to launch during a PM leadership transition with no full owner to carry GTM and release readiness. In a category where competitors had known sync-inconsistency problems, the opening was trust.

Action

Covered pseudo-PM scope through the transition: led pricing analysis, contributed to naming, and owned release planning and sequencing. Designed and recruited the beta for customer fit, and anchored messaging on data reliability as a deliberate counter to competitors whose sync errors broke downstream campaign logic.

Outcome

Ten Phase 1 beta customers and a 25-customer Phase 2 waitlist, positioned and sequenced for a trust-led GA.

Problem

A conversational AI platform needed channel growth through global systems integrators (Infosys, Tech Mahindra, IBM Consulting, Cognizant), relationships that produce nothing without co-marketing and enablement sellers actually use.

Action

Built co-marketing and demand-gen across the SI and ISV channel and ran full-funnel solutions marketing, digital video, print, and live events, plus the sales and partner enablement that made the motion repeatable. Launched GTM campaigns in retail and financial services featuring Adobe and Google integrations, and ran a monthly partner newsletter.

Outcome

Impacted $5.3M in channel pipeline, drove a record 33% year-over-year partner-channel growth, and helped secure an 8-figure deal with a large health insurer. Newsletter readership rose 25% with an 8-point lift in click-through.
